CitSci Project Overview

  • 26 October 2025

Forest Service Most Wanted Wildlife

Citizen Science Monitoring - Southwest Conservation Corps

Introduction

Public lands are crucial in connecting citizens with natural resources, fostering resilient ecosystems, and maintaining biodiversity. The US Forest Service works to maintain these public resources through land use programs, regulated recreation, and land management, among other things. Together these programs aim to maintain sustainable land use and improve ecosystem functions in a dynamic landscape.

In creating and enacting a holistic land management plan, it is crucial for management actions to be driven by data about native species and ecosystems. In order to understand how species are impacted by management and maximize benefits to critical flora and fauna, the Wildlife department of the San Juan National Forest works to collect data on a number of sensitive species. They focus on establishing distribution and long term trends in protected species and species whose habitat is widespread in the forest.

However, due to limited personnel in the wildlife department, having additional sources for wildlife data could be highly beneficial in creating well-informed habitat management decisions for a broader array of species. Citizen science data on sensitive species in the park could serve to both supplement Forest Service monitoring efforts and begin to establish baseline distributions for understudied species.

San Juan Forest Service Monitoring Efforts. Left: Milkweed recorded during inventory for Monarch conservation efforts. Middle: Acoustic surveys for raptors in project areas. Right: Abert’s squirrel with a ponderosa cone core used as a sign of presence for long term monitoring.

Project Lead

This project is led by Makenna Reilly (contact: reillymakenna@gmail.com) as part of an independent research project for Southwest Conservation Corps. Makenna works as a seasonal wildlife team member for the Columbine Ranger District of the San Juan National forest, employed through Southwest Conservation Corps. Naturalist data for this project, while collected independently, will be shared with the Forest Service to aid in wildlife management planning and research efforts.

Methods

This project aims to compile historic observations of sensitive species in the San Juan National Forest (particularly in the Columbine Ranger District) based on records from iNaturalist. Recently collected data points can be used to create focal locations for Forest Service in house inventory and monitoring. Additionally, the project will work with volunteer citizen scientists from non-governmental organizations familiar with specific taxa to expand the collection of opportunistic distribution data for uncommon or less observed species.

Volunteers will be provided a list of relevant target species, and collect evidence of species presence (photos, audio recordings, prints, etc.), adding them as observations on iNaturalist. Location data will be automatically collected, allowing observations in the forest to be identified and reviewed before being added to an arcGIS taxa specific map.

Target species for citizen science monitoring include threatened and near threatened species, as well as species protected in the Forest Service forest management plan. Additional species of interest include hosts of vulnerable species (ex. milkweed; host for monarch butterflies), obligates of major habitat types in the forest (ex. Abert's squirrel; ponderosa pine obligate) or species whose range largely overlaps with the forest districts.

Posters with species specific information about seasonality, habitat, suspected range, and identification tips will be distributed to volunteers. Posters will include links to the project overview, as well as instructions on uploading observations. Volunteers may also join the iNaturalist project “San Juan Most Wanted Species”, however this is entirely optional and is not necessary for observations to be recorded as part of the project. A preliminary list of species of interest, and an example poster are below.
